Proposed organization: Municipality, government agency, public university, library or other public body with a comms/web team and a formal DPO

What they are doing: Running the public website's statistics for content and service decisions while passing DPO review, FG/AVG audits and public accountability questions about third-party trackers

What they need: Visitor statistics that process no personal data, store nothing on the device and are hosted in the EU, so they can run without consent and survive a regulator or journalist scan

User: Web editor / online comms adviser who reads the stats weekly; sometimes an external web supplier implements

Decision maker: Head of communications or information manager, with the DPO/FG and procurement as gatekeepers; budget approved against a framework rather than a card

Current workaround: Google Analytics loaded before consent (status quo, now publicly flagged), Matomo (self-hosted or EU cloud), Piwik PRO, Siteimprove, Govanalytics, or statistics switched off entirely

Relevance hypothesis: Hypothesis: a published scan of all 342 Dutch municipal websites turned this from a latent risk into a named, dated, press-covered finding per organisation, and the same legal commentary says a public body cannot use a cookie wall — which removes 'just add a banner' as the cheap fix and forces a cookieless tool choice

Proposed trigger: Being named in a compliance scan or press item, a council question (raadsvraag), a DPO/FG audit, or a redesign tender where the privacy annex requires no pre-consent third-party loading

Open on: Lead with what the DPO has to sign: no cookies, no localStorage, no IP storage, no fingerprinting, country-level location only, EU hosting in the Netherlands, open-source client scripts — and flag the AI chat as an opt-in with a data-transfer consequence rather than bundling it into the EU-hosting claim.

They say no because: Procurement and our DPO will want an EU vendor with a DPA, a DPIA already done and ideally a government-specific product — you are a small Amsterdam company with SOC 2 'in progress', and there are Dutch tools built for government.

Commercial hypothesis: New candidate from this round. Demand evidence: the Nixon Digital 2026 scan of all 342 Dutch municipal websites (61% load Big Tech pre-consent, 70 load Google Analytics pre-consent, 220 have no cookie banner, only 31 use a professional CMP), reported independently by ib-p.nl, security.nl, ICTMagazine and Ius Mentis ('over 60% of municipal websites share data with Google illegally'); clickport.io states Article 11.7a(5) Telecommunicatiewet bans cookie walls outright for public-sector websites and that cookieless audience measurement meeting the exemption needs no consent, making it defensible to a DPO or an AP review. Counterevidence, material: Govanalytics is purpose-built for Dutch government (€129/month) and the NL commentary recommends Matomo, Piwik PRO, Siteimprove and SIM Analytics by name — an incumbent-vendor field with existing public-sector references; Simple Analytics has no SOC 2 certification, and no DPA, DPIA template or uptime SLA appears in the supplied material, which is usually mandatory in public procurement. Note: the named public-sector logos in the brief (GOV.UK, Scottish Government, Bank of England) were not verified in this round.

The trigger is dated, named and quantified: a scan of all 342 Dutch municipal websites found 207 of 339 loading Big Tech before consent, 70 loading Google Analytics pre-consent, and only 31 using a professional consent platform.
Independent legal commentary states plainly that this is not permitted because personal data goes to the US without a clear GDPR basis, which is what forces a tool decision rather than a banner decision.
A practitioner voice in the comment thread defends the underlying need — analytics to see whether information provided to residents is actually read and how to improve it — so switching off is not costless for the web team.
A third source establishes the legal route this product fits: cookieless analytics meeting the audience-measurement exemption criteria need no consent under the Telecommunicatiewet, and the regulator treats cookie walls as invalid consent.
Product truth establishes the capabilities the DPO sign-off turns on: no cookies or browser storage, no IP collection or storage, no fingerprinting, country-level location only, EU/NL hosting, open-source client scripts, and non-EU transfer only on opt-in features.
The population is enumerable and public (342 municipal websites, with the pre-consent Google loaders identified by scan), so targeting does not depend on inference.
