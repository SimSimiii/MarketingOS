## 1. What a campaign request contains, what the Strategist produces per email, and what each role (writer, critic, cold reader, judge, sequence reader) does, with its prompt file

### Takeaway
A campaign is the user's own sentence (rendered verbatim into every prompt) plus a few structured fields; code parses the email count and the objective/relationship, then one Strategist call produces one `EmailBrief` per email (job, one idea, four-beat argument, ≤3 evidence ids, objection, must_not_say, tone, CTA "decision bridge", subject strategy). Each email then goes through a fixed loop: N candidate drafts → 15 free deterministic checks → cold-reader panel (never sees the brief) → pairwise duel → critic (sees everything) → rewrite, with one allowed pivot, then a subject-line bake-off and a whole-sequence read. There is **no language field and no tone field on the request**: the copy language is inferred by the writer from the request, and tone is set per email by the Strategist.

### Cited Findings

**The request and its context**
- `CampaignRequest` fields: `name`, `request`, `product_description`, `product_url`, `target_market`, `goals`, `sender_name`, `sender_role`, `channel` (LinkedIn), `cta_url`, `intent` — [request.py:22-48](backend/app/marketing/request.py). The docstring says `request` "is the contract and is rendered verbatim at the top of every prompt in the run" — [request.py:11-14](backend/app/marketing/request.py).
- Sender identity is a deliberate conversion lever: without it every email signs "- the X team", "the signature of a marketing department and is read as one" — [request.py:28-35](backend/app/marketing/request.py).
- The public API for creating a campaign accepts `name`, `request`, `brand_id`, `audience_segment`, `policy_preset`, `email_tier`, `model_overrides`, `sender_name`, `sender_role`, `cta_url` — no language, tone, audience-stage or send-cadence field — [.agents/skills/campaign-optimization/SKILL.md:84](.agents/skills/campaign-optimization/SKILL.md).
- `CampaignIntent`: objective ∈ {announcement, acquisition, activation, recovery, nurture, custom}; reader relationship ∈ {unknown, cold, subscriber, prospect, customer}; optional `announcement`, `reason_now`, `desired_action` — [intent.py:8-21](backend/app/marketing/intent.py). Each objective injects a rule, e.g. recovery: "Do not invent a discount, deadline or reason they stopped"; acquisition: "Do not invent the recipient's pain, installed tools or past failures" — [intent.py:35-42](backend/app/marketing/intent.py).
- When not supplied, intent is inferred by regex, including a few French cues ("panier abandonn", "lancement", "annonce", "à froid") — [intent.py:46-62](backend/app/marketing/intent.py).

**Kinds of deliverable (cold vs list, single vs sequence, channels, languages)**
- Closed set of deliverables: `EMAIL_SEQUENCE`, `SINGLE_EMAIL`, `LINKEDIN_MESSAGE` — [contract.py:81-96](backend/app/marketing/contract.py). - Count words are English only ("one"…"twelve", "a", "an", "single") — [contract.py:29-33](backend/app/marketing/contract.py); sequence hints are English ("sequence", "series", "campaign", "flow", "drip", "nurture", "onboarding") — [contract.py:69](backend/app/marketing/contract.py).
- Broadcast vs one-to-one decides layout: "A launch, an announcement, a promotion, a cart reminder" get HEADLINE + EYEBROW; "A cold email, a follow-up, anything one person would actually type to another leaves both empty" — [writer.md:341-361](backend/prompts/writer.md). HTML has two tiers: PLAIN (default, "for cold and one-to-one sales mail") and BRANDED ("for announcements and newsletters") — [render_html.py:23-31](backend/app/marketing/render_html.py), [render_html.py:110-116](backend/app/marketing/render_html.py).
- Language: "Follow the requested output language first. If none is specified, use the language of the request." — [writer.md:36](backend/prompts/writer.md); the writer emits a `LANGUAGE:` tag ("fr, en or en-GB") — [writer.md:389](backend/prompts/writer.md); HTML `lang` comes from that tag or `langdetect` — [render_html.py:309-314](backend/app/marketing/render_html.py).
- The default production model was chosen because "a two-email French support sequence scored 87/100 after source and browser review" — [policy.py:107-115](backend/app/marketing/policy.py). LinkedIn messages default to French — [schemas/linkedin.py:233](backend/app/schemas/linkedin.py).

- The product writes drafts only; it does not send: "the application does not send messages" — [README.md:40-42](README.md); "Generate drafts only: no sending" — [SKILL.md:211](.agents/skills/campaign-optimization/SKILL.md). Yet the writer is told "What you write gets sent: the user pastes it into their email tool and hits send" — [writer.md:1-3](backend/prompts/writer.md).
- Markets: the golden benchmark cases are all English B2B SaaS (Notewright, Ledgerloop, Portway) — [golden.py:158-197](backend/app/evaluation/golden.py); bench controls were added to leave "the cold-B2B-SaaS voice every other fixture here is written in" (consumer, B2B services, local, nonprofit, technical, trades) — [controls.py:38-40](backend/app/evaluation/controls.py), [controls.py:222-266](backend/app/evaluation/controls.py). - Cold-reader panel = 3 readers varying **disposition, never identity** (base persona; "weigh the effort of the next step against its usefulness"; "scrutinise whether the claims earn trust") — [reader.py:360-364](backend/app/marketing/reader.py), [reader.py:404-420](backend/app/marketing/reader.py). Panel score is the median — [reader.py:181-192](backend/app/marketing/reader.py); `understood` is a majority vote — [reader.py:202-213](backend/app/marketing/reader.py).
- `pull` is now "an uncalibrated editorial assessment from 0 to 10… not an estimate of opens, clicks" — [reader.md:31-34](backend/prompts/reader.md); `PULL_THRESHOLD = 7`, "never a send prediction" — [reader.py:35-36](backend/app/marketing/reader.py); the old clicks-per-100 table is kept for "Historical serialization compatibility only" — [reader.py:38-54](backend/app/marketing/reader.py).
- A read "lands" only if reported, understood, no audience mismatch and pull ≥ 7 — [reader.py:126](backend/app/marketing/reader.py); a version ships when gates pass, the panel landed (or gave no verdict) and the critic said ship — [craft.py:232-241](backend/app/marketing/craft.py).
- Version ordering: no blocking gate > understood > relevant > pull > assigned facts carried > attributions — [craft.py:254-288](backend/app/marketing/craft.py). The duel runs only after gates, comprehension and the "loses explicit support" rule — [craft.py:1056-1139](backend/app/marketing/craft.py). Duel labels alternate to cancel position bias and "The incumbent wins ties" — [tournament.py:19-27](backend/app/marketing/tournament.py).
- Bake-off candidates use different complete propositions and four "opening moves" (useful action; what they already do and its documented limit; most concrete evidence; supported concern) — [craft.py:72-112](backend/app/marketing/craft.py); near-duplicate candidates (same first 8 words) dropped — [craft.py:114-119](backend/app/marketing/craft.py).
- One pivot per email to an untried alternative when rewriting stalls or the critic routes `argument`/`missing_material` — [craft.py:664-749](backend/app/marketing/craft.py), [craft.py:1141-1158](backend/app/marketing/craft.py). A critic research question can trigger one bounded official-source recovery and a re-plan — [pipeline.py:330-401](backend/app/marketing/pipeline.py).
- Subject bake-off: alternative must beat the incumbent by `MIN_SUBJECT_GAIN = 0.3` points — [subject_lines.py:47-54](backend/app/marketing/subject_lines.py), [subject_lines.py:170-172](backend/app/marketing/subject_lines.py).
- Presets: `fast` = 1 draft, no critic, no panel, no duel, no subject variants, no sequence pass, 1 revision; `balanced` = defaults (2 candidates, critic, panel, tournament, 4 subject variants, sequence pass, 2 revisions); `maximum` = 3 revisions, 6 subject variants, 3 sequence reworks — [policy.py:117-157](backend/app/marketing/policy.py). Every preset routes **all roles** to one model: `{"*": "gpt-5.6-sol"}` — [policy.py:114-115](backend/app/marketing/policy.py).
- Stage order in code: contract → knowledge (reused by fingerprint) → intelligence → proof preflight (may stop the run) → positioning/demand/audience → strategy → craft per email → optional material recovery + re-plan → sequence pass → report — [pipeline.py:297-402](backend/app/marketing/pipeline.py), [pipeline.py:483-570](backend/app/marketing/pipeline.py).

### Inferences
- Because the language is chosen by the writer model from the request text rather than declared, every language-specific deterministic rule would first need a reliable language signal; the `LANGUAGE:` tag emitted by the writer ([email_copy.py:236-237](backend/app/marketing/email_copy.py)) is the natural hook.
- Since `{"*": "gpt-5.6-sol"}` routes writer, reader, judge and critic to the same model, the "independent" cold reader is independent in context but not in model, so any same-model preference bias is not neutralised in production (the bench's `head_to_head` deliberately grades with a different model, [head_to_head.py:18-22](backend/app/evaluation/head_to_head.py)).
- The default customer of the pipeline is a B2B/prosumer SaaS selling to a stranger or a trial user; B2C, local and nonprofit registers exist only as bench controls.

### Gaps
- No send-time, cadence or delay between sequence emails exists anywhere (search for cadence/delay fields returned nothing in `backend/app`); the writer is told sequence position does not establish recipient activity ([writer.md:112-120](backend/prompts/writer.md)), but timing itself is not modelled.
- French number words in requests ("cinq emails") are not parsed (English-only list, [contract.py:29-33](backend/app/marketing/contract.py)); I did not run `parse_contract` on French samples, so the exact fallback (likely the non-explicit default of 3) is inferred from code, not executed.

## 2. Deterministic gates: what each one blocks or flags, with thresholds and phrase lists

### Takeaway
`gates.run_all` runs 14 checks in fixed order (structure → honesty → deliverability → clarity → variety), and the craft loop adds a 15th (`planned_call_to_action`). Ten can block; substantiation, CTA-vs-offer, claim-scope review, assembly repetition, evidence context, clarity-category and the sameness swap test are advisory. All lexical lists (stock phrases, spam terms, sameness frames, copy-review cues) are English; several regexes are ASCII-only, which a standalone replica shows produces concrete French defects.

### Cited Findings

**Design rules for gates**
- Issues are "phrased as the fix" because they are handed straight back to the writer; "a gate that blocks on taste would spend the user's budget arguing with a model" — [gates.py:9-17](backend/app/marketing/gates.py).
- Order: structure, placeholder, stock phrase, evidence, capability scope, spam, overlap, duplicate CTA, duplicate sign-off, CTA-vs-offer, clarity, substantiation, sameness, copy review — [gates.py:818-833](backend/app/marketing/gates.py); `planned_call_to_action_gate` appended in the loop — [craft.py:604](backend/app/marketing/craft.py). The functional doc still says "13 checks" — [docs/architecture-fonctionnelle.md:355-374](docs/architecture-fonctionnelle.md) (outdated count).
- Markup (`**bold**`, `> `) is stripped before any gate reads text, so a bolded fragment cannot hide a banned phrase or break a verbatim quote — [email_copy.py:143-165](backend/app/marketing/email_copy.py).

**The 10 blocking sameness frames** ("a frame, not a phrase") — [sameness.py:56-142](backend/app/market/sameness.py): naming the reader's competitor as the threat; "while you are still doing X" scold; "still doing it manually" opening; "what if I told you" hook; false "quick question" opening; "imagine" hypothetical; "most companies don't realise"; "this is costing you money"; "don't miss out / get left behind" (FOMO); "I'll keep this short". Each comes with an instead, e.g. FOMO: "Give the reason this particular reader would act this particular week" — [sameness.py:128-135](backend/app/market/sameness.py).

**Prompt-vs-check slack is a deliberate convention**: asked 45 words per paragraph, checked at 50; asked 90–200 body words, checked 60–220; bold asked ≤ 2, checked ≤ 3; bullets asked ≤ 4, checked ≤ 5; bullet width asked < 10 words, checked ≤ 16 — [email_copy.py:52-121](backend/app/marketing/email_copy.py), [writer.md:312-316](backend/prompts/writer.md), [writer.md:363](backend/prompts/writer.md). Over-wide paragraphs and too few blocks are re-broken in code at sentence boundaries without changing a word — [email_copy.py:268-313](backend/app/marketing/email_copy.py).

**Other deterministic rules outside `run_all`**
- Substantiation veto in selection: a rewrite that loses explicitly quoted support "does not take the title, whatever the ballot says" [LOOP] — [craft.py:1069-1110](backend/app/marketing/craft.py), [substantiation.py:201-204](backend/app/marketing/substantiation.py).
- Comprehension veto: a draft the panel could not decode loses to one it could at any score [LOOP] — [craft.py:343-350](backend/app/marketing/craft.py), [craft.py:1082-1083](backend/app/marketing/craft.py).
- Proof preflight: with `require_proof`, the run stops before the Strategist when there is neither third-party proof nor checkable specifics, or a blocking gap — [preflight.py:75-87](backend/app/marketing/preflight.py), [pipeline.py:525-526](backend/app/marketing/pipeline.py), [policy.py:88-98](backend/app/marketing/policy.py).
- Subject options are dropped for free if subject > 65, preview > 110 or preview = subject, and screened by all gates before being scored — [subject_lines.py:67-78](backend/app/marketing/subject_lines.py), [subject_lines.py:145-147](backend/app/marketing/subject_lines.py).
- Sequence cross-check re-runs `overlap_gate` over the finished set — [sequence.py:81-94](backend/app/marketing/sequence.py).
- HTML size check against Gmail clipping, `MAX_EMAIL_BYTES = 102_000` — [render_html.py:50-54](backend/app/marketing/render_html.py), [campaign_orchestrator.py:1103](backend/app/orchestration/campaign_orchestrator.py).
- LinkedIn: evidence and placeholder gates block; a ~40-phrase "tells" list ("reaching out", "quick question", "i noticed you", "hop on a call", "no strings", "not a pitch"…) is advisory and buys one rewrite — [marketing/linkedin.py:38-60](backend/app/marketing/linkedin.py), [marketing/linkedin.py:184-209](backend/app/marketing/linkedin.py).

**Claims made in prompts that code does not check**
- The subject prompt lists "no colons-as-formula ("Quick question:", "Idea for you:"), no false replies ("Re:"), no brackets, no ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks, no emoji" as "Rules that are checked mechanically" — [subject_lines.md:59-66](backend/prompts/subject_lines.md). Code catches brackets (placeholder), ALL CAPS and > 2 "!" ## 3. Persuasion and copywriting principles stated in the prompts (with short excerpts)

### Takeaway
The prompts encode a coherent "direct-response for strangers" doctrine: one reader in a crowded inbox, say plainly what is being sold, one idea per email, argue (need → status quo → why it fails → mechanism) rather than assert, specifics over adjectives, proof from the ledger only, answer the objection before one small ask, subtract (`must_not_say`), short scannable layout, no stock openers or spam vocabulary, no invented knowledge of the reader, no invented urgency. Awareness stages exist as audience data with writer-facing guidance. The prompts are written in English and contain no French-specific style rules (formal "vous", typography, local conventions).

## 5. How the evaluation tools define good copy, which principles the mutations encode, and which judge blind spots are documented

### Takeaway
"Good copy" is defined three ways: (1) by construction — a hand-written or bench control must beat a copy of itself with one principle broken (judge bench); (2) against an outside referent — the system's first email duelled against a human-written control (runner/head-to-head); (3) by the operator's 0–100 human review grid. The measured gross tier is passed (32/33 judgment-only), the judge is weak on form (stock-phrase opening, wall of text), and the four HARD mutations (hollow testimonial, mechanism→benefit, second ask, proof after the ask) are defined but have no recorded result.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The evaluation layer encodes almost exactly the writer's doctrine (specifics, reader-first opening, proof, one ask, earn the ask), so it can confirm the judges see the system's own principles but cannot test principles the system does not hold (urgency, personalisation, French register, accessibility).
- The weakest measured instrument is precisely where a marketing-psychology report's "form" advice lands (stock openers, scannability); the architecture already compensates by making those gate-visible, so new form rules belong in gates, not in judge prompts.

## 6. Constraints any change must respect, and the extension points where a new rule would live

### Takeaway
A new rule must be deterministic to block, must not add model calls without updating the forecast (and its test), must not depend on user telemetry, must fit a solo developer's effort and a personal subscription's quota, and — if it touches a prompt — must keep Jinja variables, Pydantic field names and gate thresholds in sync. The code offers clear seams: a new gate function in `gates.py` + `run_all`, constants in `email_copy.py`, claim kinds in `ledger.py`, frames in `sameness.py`, cross-email checks in `sequence.cross_check`, subject screening in `subject_lines.py`, rendering in `render_html.py`, and mutations/controls in `app/evaluation/`.

### Cited Findings

- Prompts "are Jinja templates rendered with `StrictUndefined`… Field names in a prompt must match the Pydantic keys… thresholds sit next to the gate that enforces them on purpose, and a literal `{{...}}` (a merge field, say) has to sit inside `{% raw %}`" — [CLAUDE.md:274-275](CLAUDE.md) (and following lines).
- "Adding a model call to the loop means updating the forecast"; `tests/marketing/test_forecast.py` executes the pipeline at every preset and asserts the forecast contained the calls — [CLAUDE.md:295](CLAUDE.md), [forecast.py:23-27](backend/app/marketing/forecast.py). ### Inferences
- For a French-language report, the most consequential finding is that the principles MarketingOS enforces in code are enforced **for English**; for French copy, the system falls back largely to prompts and judges, plus at least one blocking false positive (accented brand names). Any claim "MarketingOS applique X" should therefore be qualified by language.
- The gaps cluster into three groups with different costs: (a) free deterministic additions that fit the architecture (subject checks, link count, avoid_words, CQ-COM promotion, French regexes, DATE claims); (b) planning fields (awareness per email, risk reversal, cadence) that need schema + prompt + normalization work but no extra calls; (c) things the architecture cannot own because the product does not send (consent, list hygiene, List-Unsubscribe headers), which can at most be surfaced as notices.
- Principles that require persuasive *placement* (proof before ask, single ask, mechanism) are exactly the HARD mutations; they are the right place to spend billed evaluation quota before claiming "applied".
