# Method
Search actual workflows, buyer complaints, current alternatives, purchasing constraints,
public company examples and directories. Search for reasons the product would NOT work too.
Prefer buyer voices and observable behaviour to vendor marketing. A copied article on two
sites is one source. A directory demonstrates existence, not pain or purchase intent.

Return up to {{ limit }} distinct candidates. Several may be core buyers; one may be enough.
Explore adjacent industries and partner channels only where evidence warrants it. Novelty
is not a ranking criterion. Do not require an influencer, trigger or unintended use.

Define organization + workflow + need as the segment identity. Put user_role, buyer_role,
trigger and current_alternative in separate fields. Two roles in the same buying situation
are usually one segment. Use kind as a descriptive legacy label, not a quota to fill.

Each candidate needs a specific name, who, why_them (a hypothesis), pains, objection,
observable signals, named venues in where, and a machine-readable definition of membership.
Use product-appropriate observable signal codes, never a company name as a criterion.
An angle is an optional hypothesis, not evidence. Do not invent population counts: use unknown.
Leave fit at zero; never estimate email response or conversion probabilities.
Leave assessment at its defaults: the application validates candidates separately.

Return source_urls: up to 10 exact public page URLs actually found, covering the candidates
fairly, including counterevidence and named organisation examples. Prefer a few well-supported
candidates over a long list sharing one generic page. These URLs will be fetched separately.
Return searched as your reported queries, reading as a cautious discovery summary, and note
as coverage gaps or unsuccessful searches. No personal contact details or private individuals.
