# What to produce

Up to {{ limit }} segments. One of them may be the audience above, in your own words, marked
`core`. **The rest must be buyers this company's own material would never have produced.**

Look, specifically, for each of these shapes. They are the shapes non-obvious demand usually
takes, and each one has its own `kind`:

- `adjacent` - the same underlying problem in a different industry, where nobody is
  marketing to them because that industry has different words for it. A tool sold to
  "e-commerce support teams" is often bought harder by clinics, by parts distributors, by
  university admissions offices - all of whom answer the same forty questions a day and none
  of whom read e-commerce blogs.
- `influencer` - not the buyer. The person one seat over who feels the pain every single day
  and has to convince the buyer. They get a completely different email: theirs is about
  their Tuesday, not about ROI.
- `channel` - somebody who would put this in front of their own clients. An agency, a
  consultant, a bookkeeper, a systems integrator, a marketplace, a franchise head office.
  One conversation, forty users. Almost always underweighted.
- `triggered` - defined by something that just happened to them rather than by what they
  are. They raised a round, they hired their third support person, they migrated platform,
  they got a compliance letter, they lost a bulk customer, their supplier discontinued
  something. This is the highest-converting kind of segment in existence and it never
  appears on anybody's homepage, because homepages describe categories and triggers are
  events. Find at least one if the product plausibly has one.
- `unintended` - people using a product like this for something its makers did not design it
  for. Search for that: the reviews and forum posts where somebody says "we actually use it
  to...". A real one of these is worth more than the rest of the map.

Where the positioning above shows a competitor already saturating a segment, say so in that
segment's `basis`. A crowded segment is not disqualified - it is a segment where the copy has
to work harder, and the strategist needs to know which one it is holding.

# What each segment needs

- **name** - short and specific. "Independent repair shops that sell refurbished stock", not
  "SMBs".
- **kind** - one of `core`, `adjacent`, `influencer`, `channel`, `triggered`, `unintended`.
- **who** - one person in a situation, in the words they would use. "A three-person shop
  doing 40 warranty questions a week by hand in a shared Gmail account." If it reads like a
  category, it is not done.
- **why_them** - why this specific product matters to this specific person. Different for
  every segment on your list. If two segments could swap this sentence, one of them is not
  a real segment.
- **trigger** - what makes someone like this start looking. For a `triggered` segment this
  is the entry; elsewhere leave it empty rather than inventing one.
- **pains** - what it costs them today, as short strings.
- **objection** - the reason this particular buyer says no. Not the generic one.
- **angle** - the one line to open an email to them on. A segment nobody can write a first
  sentence for is research, not an audience.
- **sophistication** - one of `unaware`, `problem_aware`, `solution_aware`, `product_aware`,
  `most_aware`. This decides where an email is allowed to start, so think about it: an
  `adjacent` segment is usually `unaware` or `problem_aware` even when the core segment is
  `solution_aware`, because nobody has ever pitched them this category.
- **fit** - 0 to 1. Of the people who match `who`, roughly what share would be interested
  enough to reply to a good email. Be honest and be spread out: a map where everything is
  0.4 is a map that has not made a judgment. Most real segments sit between 0.05 and 0.35;
  above 0.5 you are claiming something extraordinary and `basis` had better carry it.
- **basis** - **the most important field on this entry.** Why that number. Name the
  observable things you reasoned from: how loudly this group complains about the problem in
  public, whether anybody is already selling to them, what they already pay for adjacent
  tools, how a purchase gets approved for them, how reachable they are. Nobody has ever sent
  this campaign, so the rate is one informed opinion - and an opinion whose reasoning is on
  the page is one the person who knows this market can correct in ten seconds. An opinion
  without it is a number they will either over-trust or ignore.
- **population** - roughly how many exist, in whatever units your evidence supports:
  "about 8,000 listed on the association register", "a few hundred in the UK". Say "unknown"
  rather than inventing precision.
- **signals** - the observable markers that identify a member of this segment from the
  outside. What would a person look at to say "yes, that one counts"? A page on their site,
  a job posting, a tool in their stack, a listing they appear in, something on their pricing
  page. **This field is what makes the segment findable by name later**, and a segment with
  no signals cannot be prospected, cannot be advertised to, and probably cannot be targeted
  by any means at all - which is itself worth knowing.
- **where** - named places they are findable in bulk. A directory, a marketplace's seller
  list, an association register, a conference exhibitor list, a subreddit, a trade body. Real
  names, not "LinkedIn" and not "online".

Fill **searched** with the queries you actually ran, and **reading** with one paragraph on
where the demand in this market really is - the thing you would say out loud to the founder
if you had thirty seconds.

# Rules

**Search before you answer.** Look for where this problem gets complained about, which
industries buy the adjacent tools, what review sites say people use this category for, which
directories list businesses of this shape. A map assembled from memory is a map of the
training data's idea of this market, and it will read as plausible and generic in exactly the
way this whole exercise exists to avoid.

**Do not pad to {{ limit }}.** Four segments with real reasoning beat eight where half are
the same buyer renamed.

**Do not invent a market that is not there.** Some products genuinely have one buyer. If
that is the honest answer, return the core segment, say so in **note**, and explain what you
looked for and did not find. That is a finding, and it is worth more than five fictional
adjacencies.

**No personal data.** Segments are kinds of organisation and kinds of role. Never name a
private individual here.
