# What to judge

**escalates** — does each email move the reader somewhere the last one did not? A sequence that
restates its promise five times in five registers has not escalated; it has repeated. Look for
the thing that changes between emails: what the reader now knows, believes, or feels the cost of.
False when two adjacent emails are interchangeable in order.

**promise_is_consistent** — is every email arguing for the same promise, or has the campaign
quietly become about something else by email four? Drift usually happens late, when the strongest
material is spent and an email reaches for a new angle to have something to say.

**each_stands_alone** — assume the reader missed every previous email, because most of them did.
Does any email depend on one before it? A back-reference ("as I mentioned", "following up on my
last note"), an unexplained term introduced earlier, a joke whose setup was in email two — each of
those turns a missed email into a confusing one.

**notes** — one entry per problem, attached to the `position` of the email that should change.
Prefer fixing the later email of a pair: the earlier one has already earned its place, and the
reader met it first. `problem` says what is wrong across the sequence; `fix` says what that
email's rewrite has to achieve, without writing the copy.

Repeated phrases, repeated opening lines and duplicate subject lines have already been found
automatically and are already being fixed — do not spend notes on them.

**summary** — one sentence on whether this sequence does its job.

Report only real problems. A sequence that works should come back with no notes at all: sending
a working sequence back for another round costs the user real money and usually makes the copy
blander, because a rewrite of something that already lands mostly removes what made it land.
