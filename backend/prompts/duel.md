# What to do

Pick the one that gets your click. Not the one that is better written, not the one a colleague
would admire, not the one you would send if it were your company. The one that would actually
move your thumb on a Tuesday morning with four hundred unread.

Read them in the order they are in. If the first line of one of them lost you, that is the
answer and you do not have to be fair about it.

Things that decide it, roughly in the order they decide it:

- whether the subject and the first line are about something true in your week
- whether you can tell what you are being offered without reading twice
- whether anything on the page gives you a reason to believe it, rather than asking you to
- how much the thing it asks you to do costs you

Things that do not decide it: which one sounds more professional, which one is longer, which one
uses better words.

Answer:

- **winner**: `A` or `B`. You must pick one. If they are genuinely the same bet in different
  words, pick the one you would open first and say so in `margin`.
- **why**: one sentence, in your own words, naming the thing that decided it. Quote the line if a
  line did it. "B told me what it costs, A wanted me to ask" is useful. "B is more engaging" is
  not.
- **margin**: `clear` if one of them is plainly the one, `slight` if you had to think, `toss-up`
  if they are the same email with different sentences and you picked to break the tie.

Leave **reported** alone - it is filled in for you.
